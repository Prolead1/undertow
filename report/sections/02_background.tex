\section{How Concentrated Liquidity Works}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{A position as a bet on a range}

Uniswap~v3 indexes price by integer ticks. An LP picks a lower and an upper
tick, and the deposit backs trades only while the pool price stays between
them\cite{adams2021uniswap}. In protocol units the price is a raw square root,
$s=\sqrt{1.0001^{\text{tick}}}$, which increases with the tick; the human price
is $q(s)=10^{d_1-d_0}/s^2$ USDC per WETH, so a lower tick means a higher ETH
price. Inside a band $[s_a,s_b]$ the position holds
\begin{equation}
x(s)=\frac{L}{10^{d_0}}\left(\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{s_b}\right),
\qquad
y(s)=\frac{L}{10^{d_1}}\left(s-s_a\right),
\end{equation}
in token units, so its USDC value is
\begin{equation}
V(s)\;=\;x(s)+y(s)\,q(s)
\;=\;\frac{L}{10^{d_0}}\left(\frac{2}{s}-\frac{1}{s_b}-\frac{s_a}{s^2}\right).
\label{eq:value}
\end{equation}
The formulas describe a simple behaviour. As the ETH price falls, the position
sells USDC and accumulates WETH; as the price rises it does the opposite. At
the edge of the band the position holds a single asset, and once the price
leaves the band it stops trading altogether. The LP is always on the wrong side
of the move, and a passive position cannot escape it.

Fees accrue in proportion to the position's share of active liquidity.
Narrowing the band raises that share for a given deposit, which is why
concentration is attractive, but it also shortens the expected time until the
price reaches an edge. This tension between fee density and band lifetime sits
underneath every decision in the report. Figure~\ref{fig:payoff} shows the two
effects together.

\subsection{Two losses, one larger}

The first loss is familiar. Impermanent loss (IL) is the shortfall of the
position against simply holding the initial deposit. For a full-range position
with equally valued legs, if the ETH price ratio is $r=q_t/q_0$, then
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{IL}(r)\;=\;\frac{2\sqrt{r}}{1+r}-1,
\label{eq:il}
\end{equation}
which is second order in the price move; at $r=1.2$ it is $-0.41\,\%$. On v3
the same expression applies only inside the band, and outside it the position
is a one-sided holding, so IL depends on where the band was
placed\cite{loesch2021impermanent}.

The second loss is sharper. Compare the position with a portfolio that
continuously rebalances to hold the same instantaneous exposure. A second-order
expansion of the full-range value shows that the LP falls behind that
portfolio at the rate
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{dLVR}_t\;=\;\frac{1}{8}\,\sigma^2\,V_t\,\mathrm{d}t,
\label{eq:lvr}
\end{equation}
which is the loss-versus-rebalancing of Milionis et
al.\cite{milionis2022lvr}. Adverse selection therefore grows with the square
of volatility and with position size, and fee income must exceed it for passive
provision to be viable. Concentration raises fee density but not the fee rate,
so it helps only when the extra fee share outweighs the extra exposure to
arbitrage. The measurements in Section~\ref{sec:related} find that, on many
large pools, it does not. Figure~\ref{fig:losses} shows the shape of both
losses.

\subsection{The decisions and the risk}

An LP that wants to improve on the passive outcome must decide when to act,
where and how wide to place the next band, and how much market risk to carry.
The decisions interfere. A wider band delays the next costly recentring but
earns less per trade; a hedge removes directional variance but pays funding and
perp fees; acting sooner protects fee income but multiplies transaction costs.
Because acting is lumpy, the problem is one of impulse
control\cite{anchuri2026rammstein}, and because the outcome is uncertain, the
right measure is not profit but profit per unit of risk. We use two such
measures throughout: the annualised Sharpe ratio and the conditional
value-at-risk (CVaR) at the $5\,\%$ level, a coherent risk measure that can be
optimised through its convex representation\cite{rockafellar2000cvar}.
Figure~\ref{fig:decisions} makes the sequence concrete.
