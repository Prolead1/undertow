\section{Data Collection and Validation}
\label{app:data}

The study replays recorded history rather than simulating a market from a
fitted process, so the tape's provenance and its known defects are part of the
result. This appendix records the pools, the feeds, the checks that were run
and the two warnings that survived them.

\subsection{Pools and window}

Table~\ref{tab:app:pools} lists the two pools. Both are USDC/WETH pools on
Ethereum mainnet; the token ordering is fixed by address, so USDC is token0
and WETH is token1 in both, and all monetary quantities in the study are in
USDC. The window is January 2022 to December 2024, with 2024 held out. The
primary pool's higher activity is why it carries the learned policy; the

\subsection{The recorded feeds}

Each pool contributes an event tape of swaps, mints and burns; the window
contains no collects whose position was opened inside it, so the tape's
collect stream is empty and fee income is instead taken from the pool's
fee-growth accounting, the settlement path the engine replays. Five global
feeds join the tape: one-minute
reference prices, per-block gas prices, regime labels, boundary snapshots of
the tick lattice, and eight-hour perpetual funding. Table~\ref{tab:app:streams}
counts the rows for both pools. The reference and regime feeds carry two
symbols, \texttt{ETHUSDC} and \texttt{ETHUSDT}; the loader keeps one per
dataset (the deeper \texttt{ETHUSDT} feed) so no observation mixes the two
series.

\subsection{Validation and the two warnings}

Fourteen checks ran on each dataset: block ordering, key uniqueness,
monotonic timestamps, absence of future data, block contiguity, swap-sign
convention, tick and price consistency, tick spacing, liquidity conservation,
reference coverage, regime completeness, fee reconciliation, external
cross-checks and route cross-checks. Ten were conclusive, of which two
returned warnings; four could not be completed because the tape holds no
position lifecycle and no external magnitude feed.

The first warning concerns the sign convention. Of the primary pool's swaps,
$128$ of $6\,862\,602$ report non-positive or same-signed token amounts, and
$26$ of $447\,714$ do so on the contrast pool. These are subgraph artefacts
around zero-amount swaps; the replay treats a same-signed row as a transfer
of the reported magnitudes and the affected rows are a fraction of a basis
point of the tape.

The second warning concerns the edges of the reference feed. The first close
lands at $59.999$ seconds past the window's first minute and the last inside
the window at the final minute's close, so an as-of join against the exact
boundary leaves the opening and closing minute without a fresh bar. The loader
enforces the split wall and the replay warm-up covers the left edge, so no
decision inside the window is affected.

Two further limitations matter for the reported results. First, the subgraph
does not expose the pool's active liquidity on swap rows, so the replay reads
the boundary snapshot and the fee-growth stream for liquidity state instead of
the swap rows. Second, no completed position lifecycle sits inside the window,
so the fee reconciliation against collects was skipped; fee income is instead
taken from the protocol's own fee-growth accounting, which is the settlement
path the engine replays exactly.

\subsection{Splits and evaluation windows}

Training uses windows of thirty days resampled from the 2022--2023 lane, with
the final 180 days of that lane carved out for checkpoint selection. The
evaluation runs from 2024-01-01 to 2024-12-31 at midnight UTC, which tiles
into twelve full thirty-day windows and a final partial window of five days,
for thirteen episodes. Table~\ref{tab:appendix-lvr} in
Appendix~\ref{app:ablations} lists the realised market state of each evaluation
window; the window boundaries are fixed before any policy is scored, so no
comparison selects favourable periods after the fact.
