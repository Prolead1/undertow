Providing liquidity on Uniswap~v3 looks passive, but it is not. An LP chooses
a price range, earns fees while the price stays inside it, and loses whenever
informed traders move the pool price against the quoted level. Those losses are
not hypothetical: on many large pools the arbitrage losses borne by liquidity
providers exceed the fees they earn. The LP is left with three decisions, all
of them costly: when to recenter, how wide to keep the band, and how much of
the position's exposure to hedge. We study those decisions as a
risk-sensitive reinforcement learning problem, in a testbed built on two years
of recorded on-chain trades. An exact replay engine provides the ground truth,
a fast simulator provides the training throughput, and a parity harness keeps
the two honest. The report asks four questions. Where does fee income fall
short of the losses? Why does a standard learning algorithm fail on this task,
and what repairs it? Can a single policy manage timing, width and hedging
together and outperform the best rules written by hand on unseen data? And
under which market environment does active management begin to pay? \pending{The final numbers and the last two answers
will be filled in once the experiment runs complete.}
