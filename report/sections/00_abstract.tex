Providing liquidity on Uniswap~v3 looks passive, but it is not. A liquidity
provider (LP) chooses a price range, earns fees while the price stays inside
it, and loses when informed flow moves the price against it. On many large
pools those losses exceed the fees earned. The LP must decide when to
recenter, how wide to keep the band and how much to hedge; each choice costs
gas, fees or slippage.

We study those decisions as a risk-sensitive reinforcement learning problem
on three years of recorded on-chain trades from the $0.05\,\%$ (500 pips)
ETH/USDC pool, with 2024 held out and frictions charged. The study asks
where fees fall short of losses, why a standard learner fails, whether one
policy can manage the band and the hedge jointly, and where active
management pays.

Fees fail to cover impermanent loss at every width on the primary pool; the
$0.30\,\%$ tier turns the wide bands net positive, while rebalancing loses on
both. A standard learner collapsed into churn; after repairs, five seeds
learned to deploy one band and hold. The
mean learned policy fails the pre-registered Sharpe test against the frozen
best static width ($-0.73$, CI $[-1.36,-0.04]$) and earns less, but cuts
CVaR against every unhedged rule ($+23.7$ to $+34.2$). A delta-matched
hedge cuts the tail to $-3.15$ from $-59.65$ for $2\,731$ per window. The
best seed ties the static width on Sharpe and leads on tail risk. Active
management buys insurance in stress and pays for it in calm.
