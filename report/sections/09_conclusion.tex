\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Concentrated liquidity promises more fee income while the price stays inside a
chosen band, and the same position that earns those fees is the position that
loses to informed order flow. An LP who wants to do better must decide when to
recentre, how wide to stay and how much risk to hedge, and those decisions are
costly, uncertain and dependent on one another.

This study asks whether a risk-sensitive learned policy can make those
decisions jointly better than the best hand-written rules, and where active
management stops paying. Answering that required costs to be measured rather
than assumed, so we built a testbed from three years of recorded trades, with
an exact replay engine for ground truth, a fast simulator for training and a
parity harness between them. The questions were where fees fail, why the first
policies failed, whether one policy does better, and where active management
pays.

The measured verdict is narrower than the bargain promised. On the
$0.05\,\%$ pool in 2024 the liquidity leg of the rule ladder finished below
the pure buy-and-hold basket, and the rebalancing rules lost the most. The
learned policy deployed one band and held it without learning to time, failed
the Sharpe clause of the pre-registered rule against the frozen comparator
(difference $-0.733$, interval $[-1.355,-0.041]$), and passed the tail clause
against the unhedged rules. Its best seed matched the frozen comparator on
Sharpe and led it on CVaR, while the delta-matched hedge bought a nearly flat
tail at a carry cost. A higher fee tier turned the wide bands net positive
while the rebalancing rules stayed behind, so the sign of passive provision is
set by the fee tier rather than by the choice of rule. Learning did not make
active management pay on this market. What the policy bought was defence, and
the delta-matched hedge put a price on it.

What remains open follows from the boundary the windows draw. The action space
can admit continuous width and an explicit exit decision, the learned policy
can be trained on the $0.30\,\%$ fee tier, and the repairs can be probed one
at a time in a factorial ablation. Latency, private order flow and the exact
funding of a real venue can be modelled rather than proxied. Those runs share
the testbed, and the remaining question is whether the learned policy pays in
a market that charges more per swap or moves more slowly.
