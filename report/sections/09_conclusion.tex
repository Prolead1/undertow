Concentrated liquidity provision is a problem of impulse control under
adversarial order flow, and the report's central question is whether a
risk-sensitive learned policy can jointly manage band timing, band width and
directional risk better than the best rules tuned by hand once gas, slippage,
fees and funding are charged. To make that question answerable rather than
rhetorical, this report has described Undertow: an exact on-chain replay
backtester, a fast simulator checked for parity against it, a risk-sensitive
formulation of the LP problem, and a protocol that reports Sharpe and CVaR
alongside raw PnL. The four claims are ordered so that the negative result
comes first, because a policy can only be judged against an honest baseline:
C1 bounds the economics, C2 explains why naive learning fails there, and
C3--C4 ask what a policy that survives can add.

Future work follows directly from the boundaries of the study: extending the
joint action space with continuous width and optimal exit, running the boundary
sweep across more pairs and fee tiers, modeling latency and private order flow
explicitly, and replacing the representative perpetual proxy with a model of
funding and basis for a specific venue. Each extension runs the same testbed
again rather than redesigning it.
