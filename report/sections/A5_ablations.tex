\section{Ablation and Boundary Tables}
\label{app:ablations}

The design section lists the probes that complete the study; this appendix
carries their full tables. All tables are generated from the evaluation
records by the reproduction program, so the numbers can be checked line by
line against the analysis artefacts.

\subsection{Static width selection}

The protocol freezes the static width with the best Sharpe ratio on the
training split. Table~\ref{tab:appendix-static-train} reports the three widths
on the training windows and Table~\ref{tab:appendix-static-eval} their
evaluation counterparts. The width of $50$ tick spacings wins the training
selection, and its evaluation row is the frozen comparator used in the main
text; the width of $250$ would have scored better on the evaluation year,
which is exactly the selection risk the pre-registered rule guards against.

\subsection{The scripted recentring rules}

The ladder's two active rules complete the comparison. Both recentre the band
when the price moves; one does so whenever the price leaves the live band, the
other at most daily and only when the estimated fee gain covers gas. Table
\ref{tab:appendix-extra} reports their means, and their per-window rows are in
Appendix~\ref{app:results}. They are the empirical case against timing on this
pool: high fee income, larger realised losses.

\subsection{Friction ablation}

The friction ablation removes one cost term at a time from the same backtest
ledger, so the differences are exact. Table
\ref{tab:appendix-friction-wide} runs it on a one-shot band, Table
\ref{tab:appendix-friction-tau} on the recentring rule, and Table
\ref{tab:appendix-friction-contrast} repeats the first on the higher fee tier.
Removing impermanent loss moves the one-shot band by thousands of USDC and the
recentring rule by tens of thousands, while gas and slippage together move the
one-shot band by less than forty. The cost that decides the result is the loss
to arbitrage, not execution.

\subsection{Fee-tier contrast}

Table~\ref{tab:appendix-contrast} repeats the whole rule ladder on the
$0.30\,\%$ pool. The fee tier is the one axis that flips the sign of the
passive arithmetic: the wide, full-range and static bands finish ahead of the
opening basket, while both recentring rules still lose several thousand USDC.
The contrast pool's tape is smaller and its regime path is not identical to
the primary pool's, so the comparison is between fee tiers on the same
recorded year, not a controlled experiment on one tape.

\subsection{Boundary sweep}

The sweep reruns the ladder at a quarter and four times the recorded gas cost
and at a tenth and ten times the capital. Table~\ref{tab:appendix-lvr} first
records the market state of each window: annualised realised volatility,
swap notional, and the analytical full-range LVR floor. The four level tables
then report the reruns. Two findings stand out. First, no gas price or capital
level turns either recentring rule positive; cheap execution alone does not
revive timing, and a larger book scales fees and losses together. Second, the
best validation seed's (seed 3) Sharpe ratio stays between $1.40$ and $1.46$
at each level, so its risk-adjusted result is not an artefact of the tested
cost range.

\subsection{Regime-feature ablation}

The final probe retrains the same recipe across the five seeds with the regime
slot of the observation frozen to a constant. Table
\ref{tab:appendix-noregime} reports the arm. Its mean Sharpe is $0.837$ against
$0.563$ with the live feature, a paired difference of $+0.274$ whose interval
$[-0.13,+0.68]$ includes zero, while its mean CVaR is \usdc{-32.89} against
\usdc{-27.63}, a difference of \usdc{-5.26} with interval $[-6.21,-4.22]$ that
excludes zero. The live label therefore buys tail protection without a
detectable return cost. The arm also diverges more across seeds: one seed
never deploys, two widen to the $1000$-spacing band, and the seed with the
highest validation score finishes worst out of sample.
